# Gifts and Awards Policy

A **gift** is anything bought to give to someone else, whatever its item category — a bracelet
bought for a client is a gift as well as jewelry. An item is a gift when the receipt is declared as
Client Gifts or the line names who it is for. Food, drink and gratuities consumed at a meal are
not gifts under GIFT-01, even when a guest is named — the meal rules govern them. GIFT-04 is
different: it covers meals as well as gifts.

**GIFT-01 — Client gift cap.** Gifts to clients and prospects are capped at **$50 per recipient
per occasion**. An occasion is one receipt; lines on it that name the same recipient are added together for the cap,
and the $10 test applies to each line. Spend
above the cap is a **medium** exception. Any gift of $10 or more must also name its recipient, in the line description or elsewhere on the expense; an unnamed gift at that value is a **medium** exception.
Cards and small tokens under $10 need no named recipient. Gift cards and other cash equivalents
fall under GIFT-02 alone and are not also tested here. Unnamed gifts are each tested on their own,
and when both parts of GIFT-01 apply, one exception cites every line involved.

**GIFT-02 — Gift cards and cash equivalents.** Gift cards, prepaid cards, vouchers, and any
other cash equivalent are **never** reimbursable, at any amount and for any recipient. This is a
**high** exception.

**GIFT-03 — Employee gifts and awards.** Recognition gifts for employees must be processed
through HR and are not reimbursable on an expense report. This is a **medium** exception.

**GIFT-04 — Government officials.** Gifts, meals, or anything of value provided to a government
official, public employee, or state-owned enterprise employee are **prohibited** under our
anti-corruption policy, at any amount. The exception cites every line provided to the official — including a meal line that
also covers the employee's own meal, but not a gratuity (EXC-04) — and replaces GIFT-01 for those lines. Other rules, such as
the meal cap, still apply to the receipt. This is a **critical** exception and is routed to Compliance immediately,
never to the standard audit queue.
